# Steal The Best
## What competitors do well → what VLTD takes → how to do it

---

## ZERO COST — Do Now (copy, messaging, no dev work)

---

**Scanlily — Pain-point marketing**
They do: Lead with "LOST IN YOUR OWN COLLECTION CHAOS?" — hook on the feeling, not the feature.
Steal: Rewrite VLTD's ad copy and onboarding headline to open with the pain. "You have thousands of dollars of stuff scattered across boxes, spreadsheets, and memory. VLTD is where it all lives."
Cost: Zero. Rewrite copy.

---

**VeVe — "Collecting Journey" language**
They do: Frame collecting as an ongoing story, not a static list. "Track your Collecting Journey."
Steal: Use journey language everywhere — onboarding ("Start your vault"), milestones ("Your collection hit $5K"), sharing ("See their vault").
Cost: Zero. Change the words already in the app and marketing.

---

**artscapy + Cardo + Card Pilot — Investment/portfolio framing**
They do: Call the collection a portfolio. Use words like appreciation, allocation, market value — not just "stuff you own."
Steal: In VLTD's analytics and marketing, always say portfolio, not collection. Say appreciation, not price change. Say vault, not list.
Cost: Zero. Terminology shift.

---

**My Vinyl Vault — Permanence language**
They do: The word "vault" signals that what you've built is lasting and serious.
Steal: You already have the name. Lean into it harder. "Your vault never forgets." "Everything you've ever collected, in one place, forever."
Cost: Zero. Marketing copy.

---

**Stadium Insurance — Insurance framing**
They do: Remind collectors that their stuff has real-world value that needs protecting.
Steal: Add one line to VLTD's value prop: "Your vault is your insurance record." Every item logged = documentation if you ever need to file a claim.
Cost: Zero. One sentence that makes VLTD more valuable without building anything.

---

## BUILD LATER — Requires Dev Work

---

**Toyzie AI — Scan to value**
They do: Point your phone at a toy and instantly get what it's worth. 100K downloads proves the behavior is real.
Steal: Camera scan that identifies an item and drops it into your vault with current market value pre-filled.
Priority: High. This is the lowest-friction onboarding path possible.

---

**Raremarq — Auto-import from external sources**
They do: One-click import of CGC certification data — no manual entry.
Steal: Import from CGC, PSA, Discogs, eBay sold history. Collector already has all this data somewhere — VLTD should pull it in, not make them re-enter it.
Priority: High. Manual entry is the #1 reason people abandon collection apps.

---

**Dead Format — Community inside the collection**
They do: Forums and social feed built around what you collect, not what you're selling.
Steal: When someone adds a rare item to their vault, that's a social moment. Other collectors should be able to see it, react to it, follow vaults they admire. Community lives inside the vault view — not in a separate tab.
Priority: Medium. Needs user base first to be valuable.

---

**ComicCollx — Education in context**
They do: Teach collectors what grades mean, what to look for, how value works.
Steal: When a collector adds their first graded comic or PSA card, surface a brief explainer: what the grade means, how it affects value, what a 9.8 vs 8.0 is worth. Educational moments inside the product build trust and reduce churn.
Priority: Medium. High value, medium complexity.

---

**Cardo + artscapy — Financial-grade analytics**
They do: Charts, comps, historical pricing, appreciation over time — real investment intelligence.
Steal: VLTD's portfolio view should feel like a Bloomberg terminal, not a spreadsheet. Total vault value, value over time graph, top appreciating items, category allocation breakdown.
Priority: High. This is what separates VLTD from every list-based tracker.

---

**Blue Dots — Storage/location tracking**
They do: Where is the item physically? Which box, which shelf?
Steal: Add a location field to every item — Displayed / In Storage / Loaned Out / For Sale. Collectors with large collections need to know where things are.
Priority: Low. Useful but not a differentiator.

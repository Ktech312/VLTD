# Vaulted vs. ThingsBook — Competitive Brief

---

## The Short Version

**ThingsBook is not your competitor. It's a lifestyle journal.**

After researching it: ThingsBook is a social platform for documenting your personal life —
books you read, restaurants you visited, trips you took, fashion you loved. It's a digital
scrapbook of things you *experienced or consumed*, with a social feed and sharing features.

Vaulted is about things you *created or own* — and proving it legally.

These are completely different emotional needs. You're not fighting over the same user.

---

## What ThingsBook Actually Is

- A personal journaling + collection-sharing social app
- Users write long-form entries about their life, taste, and interests
- Collections include: books, meals, travel, fashion, hobbies, goals
- Social-first: public sharing, friend lists, a feed
- Think: Pinterest meets Day One meets Goodreads
- **Their tagline energy:** "A living record of who you are and what you love"

---

## What Vaulted Actually Is

- A documentation and authentication platform for artists and collectors
- Users catalog things they *created or own* with legal provenance
- Collections include: original artwork, collectibles, comics, cards, memorabilia
- Private-first: your vault is yours, not a feed
- **Your tagline energy:** "Your art. Your proof. Forever."

---

| | ThingsBook | Vaulted |

| Primary emotion | Expression and identity | Protection and proof |

---

## Why ThingsBook Cannot Easily Copy Vaulted

ThingsBook is built on *social sharing and lifestyle curation*. To compete with
Vaulted they would need to completely rebuild their product around:

- Legal documentation and provenance
- Authentication and fingerprinting
- Certificate generation
- Privacy-first architecture
- Artist-specific trust and protection workflows

That's not a feature add. That's a different company.

Meanwhile, Vaulted going "social" like ThingsBook would dilute the one thing
that makes it valuable: **being a trusted, private, legal record.**

---

## Where ThingsBook Has an Edge (Be Honest)

- They have a polished social feed that looks good on mobile
- They have more general appeal — bigger potential audience
- They launched before you and have reviews/traction
- Their onboarding is probably smoother right now

**What this means for you:** Don't compete on polish or breadth right now.
Compete on depth and purpose. You don't need to be beautiful for everyone —
you need to be indispensable for your specific user.

---

## Your Positioning Statement (Against ThingsBook)

Use this if you ever get asked "how is this different from ThingsBook?":

> "ThingsBook is a lifestyle journal — it's for documenting what you love.
> Vaulted is a legal record — it's for documenting what you made or own and
> proving it. ThingsBook is great for sharing your taste with the world.
> Vaulted is for the moment a gallery questions your provenance, a buyer
> wants documentation, or someone disputes who created something.
> One is a scrapbook. The other is evidence."

---

## The Real Competitors to Watch

ThingsBook is not your threat. These are closer to your actual competitive space:

**1. CatalogIt** — museum and private collection management software
- More institutional, more expensive, not artist-first
- Your edge: simpler, cheaper, built for independent artists not museums

**2. Artwork Archive** — portfolio and collection management for artists
- Closer to Vaulted but subscription-heavy and not mobile-first
- Your edge: easier onboarding, smart scanning, certificate generation built in

**3. Magnus Art** — art market app with price database
- More of a market intelligence tool than a documentation tool
- Your edge: private vault, creator-focused, not just for buyers

**4. The Physical Binder / Nothing** — what most artists actually use today
- This is your real competitor
- Your edge: everything

## How to Use This Competitively

### In your messaging:
Never mention ThingsBook by name. Don't give them the air. Instead, position
against the *category* they represent:

> "Vaulted isn't a lifestyle app. It's not about sharing what you love.
> It's about protecting what you made."

### In artist outreach:
If an artist mentions ThingsBook:
> "ThingsBook is cool for life journaling — but it won't help you if someone
> disputes who made your work, or if a gallery wants provenance.
> That's what Vaulted does."

### In collector outreach:
ThingsBook won't even come up. Collectors have no use for a lifestyle journal.
This is pure Vaulted territory.

---

## The Strategic Opportunity ThingsBook Revealed

Here's the real insight from their success: **people want to catalog their life.**
ThingsBook proved that appetite exists. They're just serving the casual end of it.

You're serving the end where it actually matters legally and financially.
The collector who spent $4,000 on a signed comic doesn't want a lifestyle journal.
The artist whose piece got resold without her credit doesn't need a scrapbook.
They need Vaulted.

**Your market is smaller than ThingsBook's — and worth far more per user.**
That's exactly where you want to be as a zero-budget solo founder.

---

## One-Line Summary

ThingsBook = "I love collecting memories of things."
Vaulted = "I need to prove I own or made this thing."

Go deeper on proof. That's where you win.
